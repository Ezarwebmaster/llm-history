## What happened
At the 97th Meeting of the Acoustical Society of America at MIT in June 1979, Jonathan Allen, Sharon Hunnicutt, Rolf Carlson, and Björn Granström presented MITalk-79, capping a decade of text-to-speech research. Instead of stitching together recorded human speech, the system converted unrestricted English text through a nine-stage rule pipeline: normalizing abbreviations and symbols, decomposing words against an 11,000-entry morph lexicon (a dictionary of word-building units with pronunciations and parts of speech), applying letter-to-sound rules for unfamiliar words, parsing syntax, predicting stress, pitch contours, and segment durations, and driving Dennis Klatt's digital formant synthesizer (a mathematical model of vocal-tract resonances).

## Why it matters
MITalk-79 demonstrated a complete, modular text-to-speech pipeline for unrestricted text and directly seeded 1980s commercial synthesizers including Klattalk and Digital Equipment Corporation's DECtalk.
