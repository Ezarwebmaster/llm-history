## What happened
In September 1964, Daniel G. Bobrow at MIT completed his PhD thesis describing STUDENT, a program written in LISP that reads a restricted subset of English and solves high-school algebra word problems. It turns the sentences into a set of simultaneous equations, solves them, and gives the answer in English; if it cannot solve them, it asks the user for more information. Bobrow evaluated STUDENT against other English question-answering systems of the time.

## Why it matters
STUDENT showed that a computer could understand ordinary English well enough to do useful work. Bobrow called it a first step toward natural language communication with computers.
