## What happened
In 1965, at Stanford University, Joshua Lederberg, Edward Feigenbaum, Bruce Buchanan and chemist Carl Djerassi started DENDRAL, a project to help organic chemists identify unknown molecules by reading their mass spectra, the pattern of fragments a molecule leaves in a mass spectrometer. Heuristic Dendral generated candidate structures that fit the data, while Meta-Dendral learned general rules linking spectral features to structures. The programs were written in Lisp and are considered the first expert system.

## Why it matters
DENDRAL showed that a computer could encode a human expert's knowledge as rules and use it to make scientific guesses, a pattern that shaped later expert systems such as MYCIN.
