## What happened
In February 1988, at the Second Conference on Applied Natural Language Processing in Austin, Texas, Kenneth Ward Church of AT&T Bell Laboratories presented "A Stochastic Parts Program and Noun Phrase Parser for Unrestricted Text." The paper described PARTS, a statistical program trained on the tagged Brown Corpus that uses lexical probabilities and contextual transition probabilities across three-word windows to assign part-of-speech labels (such as noun, verb, or adjective) and bracket simple noun phrases in unrestricted English text, achieving 95% to 99% tagging accuracy.

## Why it matters
At a time when natural language processing relied heavily on hand-written grammar rules that often failed on real-world text, Church showed that simple statistical models trained on labelled corpora could accurately and efficiently tag unrestricted text, helping launch the empirical, corpus-based revolution in language processing.
