## What happened
At the end of a five-year DARPA speech program, Carnegie Mellon demonstrated Harpy, built by Bruce Lowerre under Raj Reddy and described in his May 1976 thesis. Instead of comparing a recording against every possible word, Harpy held what it expected to hear as one large network of word sequences and searched only a few of the most promising paths through it. That made it fast enough to handle connected speech over a vocabulary of 1,011 words. Carnegie Mellon calls it the world's first voice-based virtual assistant.

## Why it matters
Limiting the search to likely word sequences made speech recognition cheap enough to run in close to real time, the idea later dictation and voice assistants were built on.
