## What happened
In November 1998, in Proceedings of the IEEE, Yann LeCun, Léon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio and Patrick Haffner compared methods for recognizing handwritten characters on a standard digit task. Convolutional neural networks, which scan an image with small shared filters so they cope with shifts and distortions, outperformed every other technique tested. The paper also describes a bank-cheque reader built on these networks, which the authors say was deployed commercially and reads several million cheques per day.

## Why it matters
The paper argues that a system can be trained as a whole, from raw pixels to the final answer, with minimal hand-built preprocessing, and that convolutional networks handle the variability of 2D shapes better than the other techniques it tested.
