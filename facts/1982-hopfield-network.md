## What happened
In the April 1982 issue of the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, John Hopfield, then at Caltech and Bell Labs, described a network of interconnected neurons whose connections are set once. The network stores patterns as low-energy states and, given a partial or noisy version, settles into the nearest stored pattern. Hopfield borrowed the language of physics, describing the network's behaviour as moving downhill on an energy landscape.

## Why it matters
The "Hopfield network" gave neural networks a clear model of associative memory and helped revive interest in the field before back-propagation and modern deep learning.
