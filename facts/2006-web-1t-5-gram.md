## What happened
On September 19, 2006, the Linguistic Data Consortium released Web 1T 5-gram Version 1, a dataset contributed by Google. It records how often every English word sequence of one to five words appears in publicly accessible web pages, covering about one trillion tokens. The counts let researchers build and test statistical language models, the kind behind the machine translation and speech recognition of the day, without access to Google's raw web text.

## Why it matters
Before neural models learned word meanings from raw text, language models worked by counting short word sequences. A public table of counts at web scale showed researchers how large such training data could get, and it fed language-model work for years.
