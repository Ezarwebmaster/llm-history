## What happened
On November 20, 2026, Andrea Gregor de Varda, Ferdinando D'Elia, Greta Tuckute, Evelina Fedorenko, and Meenakshi Khosla posted a preprint testing whether individual coordinate axes (single unit dimensions) in large language models carry recurring meaning or are arbitrary. Analyzing 12 language models across six model families, they compared each model's native axes against randomly rotated axes that preserve identical overall geometry. Native axes aligned more reliably with other models' representations, interpretable linguistic features, and human language-network brain responses (measured with fMRI, a brain-imaging method tracking blood flow) than random rotations, though still well below the maximum alignment possible under unconstrained linear mappings.

## Why it matters
Challenging the assumption that neural network coordinate bases are arbitrary, the study shows LLM representations are partially canonical, requiring theories of language representation to explain both population geometry and recurring individual-unit profiles.
