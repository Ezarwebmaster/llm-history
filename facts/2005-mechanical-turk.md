## What happened
On November 2, 2005, Amazon announced Mechanical Turk, a web-services API through which a computer program can post small tasks, collect answers from people paid per completed task, and feed the results back into its own processing. Amazon called the idea "Artificial Artificial Intelligence": a program looks smart because hidden people handle the parts machines still fail at, such as spotting objects in a photograph. The name comes from an eighteenth-century chess "automaton" that secretly hid a human player.

## Why it matters
Paid micro-task crowdsourcing became a standard way to build the labeled images, ratings and transcripts that supervised learning needed, so data work like this quietly sits under much of later AI.
