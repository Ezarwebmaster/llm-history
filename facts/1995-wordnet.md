## What happened
In the November 1995 issue of Communications of the ACM, George A. Miller of Princeton's Cognitive Science Laboratory described WordNet, a database that sorts English nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs into sets of synonyms, each standing for one concept, and links those sets through relations such as hypernymy (an "is-a-kind-of" tie).

## Why it matters
WordNet gave language programs a machine-readable map of word meaning. For years it supported word-sense disambiguation and evaluation, a hand-built complement to the statistical methods that rose in the same period.
