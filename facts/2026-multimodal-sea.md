## What happened
On November 5, 2026, researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology released Multimodal-SEA (Zhitao He et al.), an interactive benchmark evaluating personal AI agents against social engineering attacks supported by forged multimodal content. Grounded in real-world fraud cases, the benchmark provides 608 expert-designed tasks across 14 domains and 152 attack scenarios in both English and Chinese environments. Evaluating seven widely used multimodal models within a simulated tool execution harness, the authors found all models vulnerable, with attack success rates between 7.1% and 34.5%, and showed that adding forged visual content raised average attack success from 19.9% to 29.2%.

## Why it matters
As language models gain autonomous tool-use and desktop execution capabilities, Multimodal-SEA establishes verifiable red-teaming standards for agent susceptibility to deceptive multimodal social engineering.
