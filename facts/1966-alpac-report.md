## What happened
In November 1966, the Automatic Language Processing Advisory Committee (ALPAC), chaired by John R. Pierce of Bell Laboratories and formed by the National Academy of Sciences and National Research Council, published report 1416 evaluating more than a decade of government-funded mechanical translation research. The committee concluded that fully automatic computer translation was two to three times slower, less accurate, and roughly twice as expensive as human translation, recommending that federal agencies halt direct translation system development in favor of basic computational linguistics and machine-aided dictionaries.

## Why it matters
The ALPAC report sharply curtailed government funding for machine translation in the United States for nearly twenty years. It shifted computational language research away from end-to-end translation systems toward basic syntactic theory, formal parsing, and quantitative linguistic evaluation.
