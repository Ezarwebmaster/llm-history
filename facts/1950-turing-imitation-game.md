## What happened
In the October 1950 issue of *Mind*, Alan Turing proposed replacing the ambiguous question “Can machines think?” with an “imitation game.” A human interrogator would exchange typed messages with unseen respondents and try to distinguish a machine from a person. Turing also considered objections to machine intelligence and discussed how computers might learn.

## Why it matters
The paper made conversational behavior a concrete way to investigate machine intelligence. Its imitation game, later called the Turing Test, became an enduring reference point in debates about artificial intelligence and helped frame the field before the term “artificial intelligence” was coined.
