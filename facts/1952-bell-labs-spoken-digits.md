## What happened
In the November 1952 issue of the *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, K. H. Davis, R. Biddulph and S. Balashek described a circuit that could recognize the ten spoken digits. Bell Labs called it Audrey. It compared the sound coming down a telephone line with stored patterns and lit the lamp for the closest match.

## Why it matters
Audrey made it possible to identify a word by comparing its sound with a reference. Even with just ten words to choose from, a change of speaker mattered. The researchers had to readjust the circuit for different voices to get reliable results.
