## What happened
In January 1951, Claude E. Shannon of Bell Telephone Laboratories published "Prediction and Entropy of Printed English" in *The Bell System Technical Journal* (Volume 30, Issue 1, pages 50–64). Extending his 1948 mathematical theory of communication, Shannon measured entropy—the average unpredictability of each symbol, measured in bits—for a 27-character English alphabet of 26 letters plus space. Instead of relying solely on short letter-frequency tables, he asked human subjects to guess each upcoming letter from up to 100 characters of preceding text. From the distribution of guesses required, he derived upper and lower bounds of roughly 0.6 to 1.3 bits per letter, showing that ordinary printed English is about 75 percent redundant.

## Why it matters
Shannon's prediction experiments linked language structure directly to next-symbol probability estimation, laying the information-theoretic foundation for language models trained to predict upcoming text.
