## What happened
In August 1960, Stanford electrical engineers Bernard Widrow and Marcian E. Hoff published "Adaptive Switching Circuits." Their ADALINE (adaptive linear) unit formed a weighted sum of its inputs and selected between two outputs. Instead of fixing its weights by hand, the system adjusted them through an iterative training process, using labeled examples and performance feedback to reduce average errors. The paper also described an electronic ADALINE prototype for classifying simple geometric patterns.

## Why it matters
ADALINE showed that learning could be implemented as repeated weight adjustment in hardware, an idea shared with later neural networks trained from examples.
