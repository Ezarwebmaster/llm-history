## What happened
In the June 1993 issue of Computational Linguistics, Mitchell Marcus, Beatrice Santorini and Mary Ann Marcinkiewicz described the Penn Treebank, a collection of over 4.5 million words of American English, including Dow Jones news stories. During the project's first phase, from 1989 to 1992, every word was labelled with its part of speech (noun, verb and so on), and more than half of the text was given a simplified outline of its grammatical structure. Annotators worked by correcting the output of automatic taggers and parsers rather than labelling everything by hand.

## Why it matters
The corpus gave researchers a large shared body of labelled text to train and test statistical language programs on, and the paper already lists projects using it to train part-of-speech taggers and parsers.
