## What happened
In the September 1967 issue of Behavioral Science, M. Ross Quillian published "Word concepts: A theory and simulation of some basic semantic capabilities," drawing on research conducted at the Carnegie Institute of Technology and the System Development Corporation (SDC). Quillian proposed modeling conceptual memory as a semantic network — a web of nodes representing word concepts connected by associative links, in which the meaning of any word is defined by its structural connections to other concepts. To test the theory, he built a computer program that recoded English dictionary definitions into this network structure, searched across paths to compare and contrast the meanings of pairs of words, and generated short English sentences describing how the concepts related.

## Why it matters
Quillian's semantic networks gave early natural language processing its foundational graph model for representing word meaning, influencing knowledge graphs, lexical databases, and later work on spreading activation in memory.
