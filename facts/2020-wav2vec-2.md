## What happened
Facebook AI posted wav2vec 2.0, a speech system that learns mostly from audio nobody has transcribed. It masks stretches of the raw waveform inside its own internal representation, then asks the model to pick the true match among several quantised candidates, so the training signal comes from the audio itself. Fine-tuning on transcribed speech turns it into a recogniser. Pre-trained on 53,000 hours of unlabeled audio and tuned on ten minutes of transcripts, it reaches 4.8/8.2 per cent word error rate on LibriSpeech clean/other. The code shipped in fairseq; the paper went to NeurIPS 2020.

## Why it matters
Transcripts, not audio, are the bottleneck for speech recognition in most languages. The paper's own result is that ten minutes of labelled speech can stand in for hundreds of hours, so masking a signal and matching it against quantised candidates became a routine way to pre-train speech systems.
