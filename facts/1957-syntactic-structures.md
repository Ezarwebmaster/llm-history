## What happened
In February 1957, linguist Noam Chomsky published *Syntactic Structures* with Mouton & Co., based on lecture notes from his courses at MIT. The monograph introduced transformational generative grammar—a system where a finite set of rules builds phrase structures and transforms them into an open-ended set of sentences. Chomsky showed that finite-state Markov processes, statistical models that pick each next word from a fixed window of preceding words, fail to represent nested clauses in natural language. He also introduced the sentence "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" to argue that grammatical structure operates independently of meaning.

## Why it matters
*Syntactic Structures* shifted linguistics away from purely descriptive and statistical approaches toward formal rule systems, shaping decades of debate between symbolic grammar and statistical language modeling.
