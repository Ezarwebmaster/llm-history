## What happened
On August 1, 2017, Bryan McCann, James Bradbury, Caiming Xiong, and Richard Socher at Salesforce Research published their paper on Contextualized Word Vectors (CoVe). Earlier systems represented each word with a fixed list of numbers (a static word vector) that stayed the same no matter the sentence around it. The authors instead trained a two-layer bidirectional long short-term memory network (a recurrent network that reads a sentence forward and backward) to translate English into German, then froze that encoder and used its outputs as context-sensitive word representations. Concatenating CoVe with static GloVe word vectors improved performance across sentiment analysis, question classification, entailment, and question answering benchmarks.

## Why it matters
CoVe bridged static word vectors and deep pre-trained language representations, showing that an encoder trained on a large translation task could supply reusable context-aware features for many downstream language tasks before ELMo and BERT generalized the idea to unsupervised text.
