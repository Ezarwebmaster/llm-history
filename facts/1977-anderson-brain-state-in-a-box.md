## What happened
In the September 1977 issue of *Psychological Review* (Vol. 84, No. 5, pp. 413–451), James A. Anderson, Jack W. Silverstein, Stephen A. Ritz, and Randall S. Jones of Brown University published "Distinctive features, categorical perception, and probability learning: Some applications of a neural model." The paper introduced the Brain-State-in-a-Box (BSB) model, a recurrent associative neural network (a network where outputs feed back into inputs over time) whose continuous activation vector is amplified by positive feedback until each unit saturates against upper and lower bounds—the corners of a hypercube "box." The authors applied the model to categorical perception of speech phonemes through distinctive features and to multi-event probability learning.

## Why it matters
The Brain-State-in-a-Box model showed how recurrent feedback and bounded neural activations turn continuous, noisy inputs into discrete categories, providing a mathematical precursor to attractor networks and connectionist speech perception models.
