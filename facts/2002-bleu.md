## What happened
On July 7, 2002, at the Association for Computational Linguistics meeting in Philadelphia, Kishore Papineni, Salim Roukos, Todd Ward and Wei-Jing Zhu of IBM Research presented BLEU. The method scores a machine translation by comparing its word sequences with one or more human reference translations, and it needs no human judge for each new output. The authors present it as a quick, inexpensive and language-independent substitute for frequent human evaluation.

## Why it matters
A cheap automatic score let machine-translation teams compare systems and training runs far more often than human evaluation allowed. BLEU became one of the standard measures of translation quality, even though it judges surface wording rather than meaning.
