## What happened
In September 1981, James L. McClelland and David E. Rumelhart of UC San Diego published Part 1 of their Interactive Activation Model in *Psychological Review*. The paper proposed a connectionist neural network with three hierarchical levels of nodes: visual features, letters, and words. Signals flowed bottom-up from features to letters to words and top-down from active words back to consistent letters, while nodes within the same level suppressed competing alternatives through lateral inhibition. This interactive feedback reproduced the word superiority effect, in which people recognize a letter more accurately inside a familiar word or pronounceable pseudoword than in isolation or a random string.

## Why it matters
The model showed that parallel networks of simple weighted units could explain contextual language perception without hand-coded symbolic rules, laying groundwork for the Parallel Distributed Processing movement and later neural language models.
