## What happened
In a letter in Nature, David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams describe back-propagation. The method repeatedly nudges the weights inside a network so that its output moves closer to the wanted answer, and it makes the network's hidden units pick up useful features of the task.

## Why it matters
It gave neural networks a workable way to learn from mistakes, and training with it went on to power most of the language models on this timeline.
