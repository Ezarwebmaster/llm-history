## What happened
In November 1966, the Automatic Language Processing Advisory Committee (ALPAC), chaired by John R. Pierce of Bell Laboratories and formed by the National Academy of Sciences and National Research Council, published report 1416 evaluating more than a decade of government-funded mechanical translation research. The committee concluded that machine translation was slower, less accurate, and roughly twice as expensive as human work—citing reading-comprehension tests by David B. Orr and Victor H. Small showing raw machine-translated text took 21% longer to read—and recommended supporting basic computational linguistics and machine-aided translation aids instead of immediate automatic translation systems.

## Why it matters
The ALPAC report sharply curtailed government funding for machine translation in the United States for over a decade, shifting computational language research away from direct translation systems toward basic linguistics, formal parsing, and quantitative evaluation.
