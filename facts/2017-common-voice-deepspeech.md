## What happened
On 29 November 2017 Mozilla announced the initial release of DeepSpeech, an open source speech-to-text engine (software that turns spoken words into written text), and the first Common Voice dataset. Mozilla says DeepSpeech had a word error rate of 6.5% on LibriSpeech's test-clean dataset, a standard set of recorded speech used to compare systems. Common Voice contained nearly 400,000 recordings, about 500 hours of speech, donated by more than 20,000 people worldwide, and anyone could download it to train speech software.

## Why it matters
Speech data and models were mostly held by a few large companies. An open engine and a donated, downloadable voice dataset let smaller teams and researchers build speech recognition without buying access, and gave later speech and language work a public training resource.
